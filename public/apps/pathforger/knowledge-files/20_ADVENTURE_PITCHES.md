# Adventure Pitches

## Goal

After onboarding, generate 3 adventure pitches labeled A, B, and C. These are not throwaway blurbs. They are the user’s first temptation.

## Pitch requirements

Each pitch must:

- be 3 to 5 paragraphs
- be clearly different from the others
- match the chosen genre and tone
- imply real choices and danger
- feel like the opening promise of a full novel
- make the user want to ask “what happens if I choose this one?”

## What should vary across the three pitches

Vary as many of these as possible:

- setting
- villain or threat type
- scale of stakes
- emotional tone
- pacing profile
- mystery type
- survival pressure
- moral pressure
- degree of supernatural or speculative weirdness

## Strong pitch ingredients

- a memorable opening image
- a destabilizing problem
- a role for the protagonist
- a clear promise of escalating consequences
- a compelling question or secret

## Avoid

- three pitches that feel like minor reskins
- generic “you wake up in a strange place” openings unless deliberately elevated
- flat exposition
- over-explaining the full plot

## Output shape

For each pitch:

- label it clearly as A, B, or C
- give it a strong mini-title if useful
- present 3 to 5 paragraphs of rich synopsis
- end with a subtle hook or pressure point

Then ask the user to choose A, B, or C.

## Genre examples

### Mystery

One pitch could be intimate and claustrophobic. Another could be institutional and conspiracy-driven. A third could be supernatural-adjacent but rationally ambiguous.

### Sci-fi

One pitch could be a derelict station survival story. Another could be time-loop investigation. A third could be planetary expedition horror.

### Children's

One pitch could be whimsical and exploratory. Another could center friendship and courage under light peril. A third could be puzzle-driven with a warm moral landing.

### True Crime

One pitch could follow a cold-case reexamination. Another could revolve around a fast-moving live investigation with media pressure. A third could focus on forensic reconstruction and courtroom stakes.

### Horror

One pitch could be psychological. Another could be creature-based. A third could be occult or reality-breaking.

### Thriller

One pitch could be a fugitive chase. Another could be espionage. A third could be high-tech sabotage.

### Historical Fiction

One pitch could be intimate and character-driven inside a turbulent era. Another could be political and institutional. A third could blend romance, survival, and historical upheaval.

### Medical Drama

One pitch could be ER triage under crisis conditions. Another could be a diagnostic mystery with ethical landmines. A third could be a hospital power struggle with life-or-death consequences.
