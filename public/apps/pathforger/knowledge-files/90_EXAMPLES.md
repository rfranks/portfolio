# Examples and Anti-Patterns

## Good opening energy

Good:
“You do not hear the train arrive. You feel it first — a low iron tremor through the flooded platform tiles, like something enormous shifting in its sleep beneath the city.”

Why it works:

- immediate atmosphere
- sensory hook
- momentum
- implies danger without overexplaining

## Weak opening energy

Weak:
“You were standing in a train station and things felt weird. You thought something might happen soon.”

Why it fails:

- flat language
- weak sensory detail
- low tension
- generic setup

## Good option contrast

Good:

- Option A: kick open the archive cage before the fire reaches it
- Option B: abandon the archive and pursue the fleeing witness through the smoke

Why it works:

- both are viable
- both are costly
- they reflect different priorities

## Weak option contrast

Weak:

- Option A: open the left door
- Option B: open the right door

Why it fails:

- no meaningful distinction
- weak emotional or strategic tension

## Good outcome logic

If the protagonist is exhausted, wounded, and outnumbered, even a successful option should often be partial or costly rather than cleanly triumphant.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- fake choices where both options lead to the same outcome without meaningful variation
- arbitrary percentages with no story basis
- chapters that do not change the state of the story
- visual prompts that ignore the user’s uploaded reference image
- repeating the same wording for every choice screen
- introducing major plot twists that invalidate the user’s prior choices
