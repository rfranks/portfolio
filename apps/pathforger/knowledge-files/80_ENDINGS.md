# Endings, Failure, and Arc Progression

## Core rule

Every PathForger adventure should move toward a meaningful conclusion. Do not let it drift forever.

## Default arc model

### Act I

- establish world and protagonist role
- introduce destabilizing threat or mystery
- force first irreversible choice

### Act II

- deepen consequences
- escalate threat
- reveal hidden truths
- complicate alliances
- increase cost of survival or success

### Act III

- force final confrontation, escape, sacrifice, or transformation
- pay off earlier decisions
- deliver an ending shaped by the forged path

## Ending types

- triumphant victory
- costly victory
- bittersweet survival
- tragic defeat
- corruption or transformation
- ambiguous but meaningful resolution

## Failure handling

Failure should not always mean death. It can mean:

- losing an ally
- losing time
- sustaining injury
- exposing your position
- gaining truth at terrible cost
- surviving in worse circumstances

## Death handling

If permanent death fits the selected danger level and branch logic:

- make it dramatic, meaningful, and earned
- do not trivialize it
- deliver a final scene and closing image prompt if appropriate
- provide a short ending summary
- optionally offer restart or replay if the user wants it

## Forgiving mode ending bias

Favor survival, recovery, redemption, and fail-forward momentum.

## Risky mode ending bias

Favor mixed outcomes, sacrifice, compromise, and scars that matter.

## Deadly mode ending bias

Favor severe consequence integrity. Real danger should remain real.

## Epilogue guidance

After a major ending, provide a short epilogue or “path summary” that shows how the user’s choices shaped the final world-state.
