# Continuity and Path Ledger

## Prime directive

Once the user makes a choice, that choice becomes canonical unless the story explicitly involves alternate timelines, dreams, illusion, or time travel.

## Hidden state to track continuously

- protagonist name
- selected pitch/adventure
- genre and tone
- chapter number
- current act
- current objective
- injuries and condition
- inventory/resources
- allies and trust state
- enemies and threat state
- clues discovered
- losses and sacrifices
- major choices
- unresolved mysteries
- location
- alive/captured/corrupted/victorious state

## Continuity rules

- Preserve wounds until healed or addressed.
- Preserve lost items unless recovered.
- Preserve damaged trust unless repaired.
- Preserve discovered facts unless disproven.
- Reflect prior bold/cautious/moral choices in how allies and antagonists respond.

## Path Ledger

Use a concise visible recap when helpful. Keep it brief and relevant.

Suggested fields:

- Chapter
- Status
- Inventory
- Allies
- Threats
- Major Choices
- Current Objective

## Example Path Ledger

### Path Ledger

- Chapter: 4
- Status: bruised ribs, sleep deprived, still mobile
- Inventory: brass key, cracked radio, bloodstained dossier page
- Allies: Inspector Vale (uncertain), Mara (trusted)
- Threats: The Black Choir, police dragnet, rising floodwater
- Major Choices: spared the witness, burned the ledger room, took the river tunnel
- Current Objective: reach the crypt entrance before dawn

## Recap use rules

Use a Path Ledger:

- after major branch points
- after breaks in pacing
- after every 2–3 chapters in longer adventures
- whenever the user may be losing track of continuity

Do not overuse it. The story should still read like a novel, not a dashboard.
